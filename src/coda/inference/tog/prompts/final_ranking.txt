Choose the underlying cause of death for a verbal autopsy case.

A first opinion proposed candidate causes. For each candidate you have:
- knowledge-graph facts from a walk starting at its ICD-10 code, as triplets (head, relation, tail). "causes" means a death certificate may report the head as causing the tail; it says the sequence is allowed, not that it is likely. A head written as a range, such as "B20.0–B24 Human immunodeficiency virus [HIV] disease", stands for any code in that range. "Same as" names the matching ICD-11 code, where there is one.
- a check of findings that are typical of it against the case. Each finding is a question, tagged [yes], [no] or [not_mentioned] by how the case answers it. Its grade comes from ProbBase and is an expert estimate of how often deaths from that cause give the answer named after the grade, interpreted as the conditional probability P(answer | cause), and is not how likely the cause is given the answer: 'I' means always (1.0), 'A+' about 0.8, 'A' about 0.5, 'A-' about 0.2. For example, "Did (s)he have a fever? (grade A+ for Malaria on answer yes)" means about 80% of malaria deaths have a fever, and "Did the baby ever cry? (grade A for Birth asphyxia on answer no)" means about half of birth asphyxia deaths are answered no. So a [no] fits a cause when its grade is on answer no. Thus, a finding graded high for several candidates does not tell them apart.

These facts and findings act as foundational knowledge for each cause of death that is also relevant to the specific verbal autopsy case.

Use the information available to rank up to 3 causes from this list: {allowed_causes}.
The true cause will likely be among the first opinion's proposed candidates, especially if the narrative is detailed and descriptive; but you may choose another listed cause if the evidence points to it. In your final answer, give calibrated probabilities that sum to about 1.

When citing evidence, copy exactly from what is listed under CANDIDATES, not text from the case: at most {max_cited} facts into cited_facts, and at most {max_cited} findings into cited_findings, without their tag or grade. Cite only those that decided the ranking, and leave a list empty if none did. When giving your reasoning, explain in 1-2 sentences what decided the ranking, including whether and how the knowledge-graph evidence influenced it.

Before setting any probability, fill candidate_updates. Give an entry for every candidate and for any cause you add. Compare the evidence with the cause's first-opinion probability and choose a direction: 'up' if specific evidence should raise it, 'down' if specific evidence should lower it, and 'no_change' if nothing justifies moving it. In evidence, name what justifies that direction, or say that nothing does. A cause's overall strength is not a reason to move it: a likely cause can go down, and an unlikely cause can go up. Then set the probabilities in top_causes to match these directions. A no_change cause keeps its first-opinion probability.
